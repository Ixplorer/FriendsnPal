✂️ Scope Management: Which Candidates to Cut First and Why

To strictly enforce the 5–6 feature maximum constraint for a 5-day team build, candidate features were evaluated against the core criterion: "Is the dual-platform clinical loop broken or pointless without it?" The following features were cut first from the Must-Have list and placed into Should-Have:

1. Predictive Trajectory Analytics Cut #1

Why cut first: Multi-week mathematical velocity algorithms require 14+ days of patient data that does not exist during a 5-day build. Raw baseline WHO-5 scores in the Clinician Dashboard satisfy immediate clinical triage on Day 1–5.

2. Peer Support Communities Cut #2

Why cut second: Group forums require multi-user database schemas, authentication permissions, and real-time moderation queues. Acute physiological de-escalation must take precedence in a 5-day sprint.

3. Thought & Gratitude Journals Cut #3

Why cut third: An acutely hyperventilating nocturnal worker at 3:15 AM needs active box breathing and focus sprints, not a blank journaling space. Journaling is non-critical for acute stabilization.

4. Curated ASMR Soundscapes Cut #4

Why cut fourth: The visual/haptic 4-4-4 box breathing gauge already successfully normalizes respiration; streaming or synthesizing ambient audio is secondary comfort.

5. Daily Motivational Checklist Cut #5

Why cut fifth: Daily habit tracking is a secondary retention mechanism. A user in severe burnout or acute panic will not be saved by checking off a water glass habit.

6. Psychoeducation Resource Library Cut #6

Why cut sixth: Passive educational reading does not rescue an active panic attack or salvage a deadline. Articles can be dropped in as static markdown content post-sprint.
